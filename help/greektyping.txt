<p spacebelow=10>Every field that you, the user, can type into will have a keyboard character at the right edge (or upper right edge for multi-line fields) of the field which indicates what characters the field will accept when you type.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>"A" means that the field accepts all input from your device's keyboard.  Whatever you type will appear in the field.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/greektyping-latin.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>"Æ" means that the field accepts only a limited set of (Latin) characters: upper and lower case Latin letters ("a" to "z" and "A" to "Z"), digits ("0" to "9"), period ("."), dash/hyphen ("-"), and underscore ("_").  Any other characters will be rejected.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/greektyping-latin-limited.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>"#" means that the field only accept digits.  Anything you type other than 0-9 will be rejected.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/greektyping-numbers.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Finally, "Ω" means that the program's built-in Greek keyboard has been enabled for this field.  The program will automatically transpose Latin letters for Greek ones.  If you type a Latin "a", a Greek Alpha ("α") will appear.  If you type a Latin "m", a  Greek Mu ("μ") will appear. </p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/greektyping-greek.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>(<i>Please note this field only transposes Latin letters into Greek ones.  Any other characters you type will appear in the field unchanged.</i>)</p>
<p spacebelow=10>As an example, look at the Search field on the Search page.  You'll see it has a Greek "Ω" at the right end of the field.  This means that the built-in Greek keyboard is enabled.  On my device's keyboard, I have typed "abcdefg", but as you can see, what appears in the field is the corresponding Greek letters, "αβξδεφγ".</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/greektyping-abc.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Moreover, in most fields, you can turn the built-in Greek keyboard on or off.  When you click in a field, if the built-in Greek keyboard can be turned on and off, then you'll see a Greek keyboard indicator over the upper right corner of the field.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>In the image below, we are creating a new Vocabulary List.  Since the built-in Greek keyboard can be turned on or off for this field, you see the Greek keyboard indicator above the upper right of the field.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/greektyping-indicator-off.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>In this case, the Greek indicator is off and the keyboard character is an "A".  This tells you that whatever you type on your device's keyboard will appear in the field.  But if you click on the Greek keyboard indicator, it will light up showing it is active, and the keyboard character becomes a Greek "Ω".  Now when you type Latin characters (a-z, A-Z), Greek characters will appear.  (<i>Any non-Latin characters you type will appear unchanged.</i>)</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/greektyping-indicator-on.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>You can see what Latin characters correspond to which Greek characters by pressing and holding on the Greek keyboard indicator (whether it's on or off) or on the "Ω" when the Greek keyboard is active.  (Pressing on the "A" does nothing.)  This brings up the "Keyboard Map" which shows you exactly how to type any Greek character.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/greektyping-map.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Here you can see which Greek characters correspond to which Latin characters.  Most of them match up naturally with their English counterparts.  If you want the Greek Rho ("ρ"), you type the Latin "r"; if you want the Greek Phi ("φ"), you type the Latin "f".</p>
<p spacebelow=10>Greek vowels can also have a number of diacritics attached to them.  You can type those by typing the appropriate "control" characters BEFORE you type the vowel.  For each diacritic, there are two different control characters, both of which do the same thing.  You can use whichever one is easier for you to type on your keyboard.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>So if you want to type an Omega with a circumflex and a smooth breathing, you would type ")" or "]" (for the smooth breathing), "=" or "3" (for the circumflex accent), and finally "w".  That will create "ὦ".  The order you type the control characters is not important; you could have typed the accent then the breathing.  But they must all be typed immediately before the vowel they are to appear on.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>Not all diacritics, however, can appear on all vowels.  As epsilon ("ε") does not take an Iota subscript, typing "!" then "e" prints only "ε".  The various control characters are only active for the vowels which can take those diacritics.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>When the Greek keyboard is active, any characters other than those which are used to create Greek characters and diacritics will be passed "as-is" to the field.  So if you type "&7" while the Greek keyboard is active, "&7" will appear in the field.  The built-in Greek keyboard only intercepts and transposes the characters you see in the "Keyboard Map".</p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you wish to type a control character so it appears "normally", then type a space immediately after it.  So if you wished to type "(" while the Greek keyboard is on, then you would type "( ".  Add a space immediately after any control character, and that character will appear in the field.  (The space after the control character will not appear.)</p>
<p spacebelow=10>Most normal punctuation, therefore, like the period, dash, comma, single quote, and semi-colon, can be typed directly into the field with the Greek keyboard turned on.  The Greek "mid-dot" is not found on Latin keyboards, so you can type it by typing the star ("*") character.  (<i>Of course, you can also turn off the Greek keyboard and directly type anything you want.</i>)</p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you click on a field labeled with "Æ" or "#", the Greek keyboard indicator will not appear.  You cannot type Greek characters into these fields. In fact, you can only type characters from the Latin alphabet here. (See the the beginning of this page for exactly which characters these field accept.)  Even if you have a keyboard installed on your device which can type non-Latin characters, the field will not accept them.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>So in the following image, our cursor is in the Account field, but the Greek keyboard indicator does not appear.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/greektyping-latin-only.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>The Search field on the Search page is another such field, although in this case, it <i>always</i> types Greek characters.  You cannot turn the Greek keyboard off.  So in the image below, our cursor is in the field, but the Greek keyboard indicator has not appeared.  The "Ω" at the end of the field tells you that this field will always type Greek.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/greektyping-greek-only.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Since there is no Greek keyboard indicator, if you wish to see how Greek characters map to Latin ones, press and hold the "Ω" at the end if the field.  As you can see in the image below, the "Keyboard Map" for this field has no control characters.  You cannot type diacritics into this field, nor do you need to.  You can, however, type wildcards as part of your search.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/greektyping-search-map.png"></p>
