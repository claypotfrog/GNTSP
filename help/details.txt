<p spacebelow=10>When you click on a particular word, either while reading the Greek text or in the Search screen, you are taken to that word's details page.  Here you see and can edit the details about this word and its use in the Greek text.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-logos.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>All words will have at least the six rows you see in the above image: part of speech and definition, number of occurrences in the Greek text, number of different forms this word can be found in the Greek text, and any notes that you have added to this word.  (Notes are entirely personal; there are none to begin with in the database.)  Finally there is a link to add this word to a Vocabulary list and a link to look up the word on different websites.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>You can click on all these lines either to edit them or to get more information.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you click on the first line containing the definition, you are taken to a screen where you can edit the gloss.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-gloss.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>The field has an "A" in the upper right corner and the Greek keyboard indicator directly above it is not enabled. Both of these indicate that the built-in Greek keyboard is turned off.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>You can enable the Greek keyboard by clicking on the keyboard indicator. (<i>On desktops, you can also press the TAB key to switch keyboards.</i>)</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-gloss-greek.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>The indicator lights up, and the "A" changes to "Ω" showing that the Greek keyboard is enabled.  See "Typing in Greek" on the "About" page for detailed information of how to use the built-in Greek keyboard</p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you make changes to the definition, click the "Save" button in the upper right to save the new definition and return to the Details screen where you will see the new gloss you just entered.  Click the back arrow in the upper left to return to the Details screen without saving any changes.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>In the same way, you can click on the Notes line to enter or edit a note about the word.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-logos-note.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Like the definition screen, you can type your notes in Greek by enabling the Greek keyboard.  And like the definition screen, click the Save link to save the note and return to the Details screen where you will see the note you just entered or click the back arrow in the upper left to return to the Details screen without saving any changes you may have made.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>If the word is a verb, then below the definition line will be a line for the principal parts of the verb.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-lego.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>The database includes the principal parts for all verbs appearing seven or more times in the Greek text.  The six principal parts are arranged in the traditional order of present, future active, aorist active, perfect active, perfect middle, aorist passive.  In addition, the imperfect will be in parenthesis after the present.  If there are multiple forms attested for that tense, then they will be separated by a slash (/).</p>
<p spacebelow=10>Please note that only forms which are attested in the Greek text are included in the principal parts.  For example, "λύω" has no perfect active forms in the Greek text, so there is no perfect active principal part listed.  Elsewhere in Greek literature, however, "λέλυκα" is the perfect active form of "λύω".</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-luw-parts.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Clicking on the Principal Parts line takes you to the screen where you can add or edit the principal parts.  (<i>Note that on this screen, unlike the others, the Greek keyboard is enabled by default.  But you can disable it by clicking on the keyboard indicator above the right corner of the field.</i>)</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-lego-parts.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>This screen operates just like the "Edit Definition" and "Edit Note" screens.  Press the Save link to save your changes; press the back arrow to cancel and return to the details screen.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>Back in the Details screen, we see that there are 330 occurrences of the word "λόγος" (in any form) in the Greek text.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-logos.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Clicking on the "Occurrences" line of the Details screen will take you to screen that lists every verse where this word appears (in any form).</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-logos-occurrences.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Here we see that the word "λόγος" appears (in all its forms) in 318 verses in the Greek text.  Clicking on any of these lines will display the text of the verse with the occurrences of the word highlighted.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-logos-show-verse.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>You can also display the text of all the verses by clicking on the arrow in the box in the upper-right.  (<i>The Plus sign you just clicked on will change to Minus.  Click on the Minus to hide the text of all the verses.</i>)</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-logos-show-all-verses.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you would like to see the English translation of the verse from the Lexham English Bible (LEB) along with the Greek text, then click the switch (or the text itself) on the right above the verses which says "Show English Verses".</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-show-eng-verses-click.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>In the image below, the "Show English Verses" switch is On (highlighted in yellow), and so we see both the Greek text and the LEB text for each verse when we expand the lines.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-eng-verses-shown.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Clicking on the "Show English Verses" switch toggles the display of any expanded verses immediately.  You do not need to refresh anything.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you wish to hide the verse text of any particular line, press and hold anywhere on the verse until the verse text closes.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you click normally on any line with the verse text showing, the program will take you to the Read screen with that book open to that chapter and verse.  All occurrences of the word you were examining in the Details screen (in any form) will be highlighted throughout the book. (You can remove the highlights by pressing and holding anywhere on the Greek Text.)</p>
<p spacebelow=10>Moreover, in the upper left corner of the Read screen next to the bookmarks icon, the back arrow will now be highlighted indicating that you can click on it.  Clicking on this arrow will return you to the Occurrences screen.  The arrow will remained enabled until you click on another word in the Greek text and examine that word.</p>
<p spacebelow=5><img src="binfile:help/images/read-with-back.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>From the Occurrences screen, you can return to the Details screen by pressing the back arrow in the upper left.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-logos.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Below the Occurrences lines, we see that the word "λόγος" has eight different forms attested in the Greek text.  Clicking on that link will bring up a screen where you can see all the different grammatical forms in which that word appears in the Greek text.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-logos-forms.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>You can then click on any of these different forms to bring up the Occurrences screen again, but this time showing you only verses where that particular form of the word appears.  As you can see here, although the word "λόγος" appears in 318 verses in all its form, it only appears in nine verses in the genitive plural, "λόγων".  The form that you are looking at appears at the top of the screen followed by its dictionary head-word in parenthesis.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>You can also see in this image that "λόγων" appears twice in Matthew 12:37.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-logos-gen-pl.png"></p>

<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-logos-gen-pl-verse.png"></p>

<p spacebelow=10>Just to the right of the "Show English Verses" button, you can also see three small ellipses buttons. <p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/occurrences_dropdown.png"></p>

<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/occurrences_dropdown_totals.png"></p>

<p spacebelow=10>Clicking on any of these lines in the menu will take you the first occurrence of that book.  In the image below we have clicked on "Hebrews" and so the first verse of Hebrews in which this word appears is at the top of the display.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/occurrences_dropdown_hebrews.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Back on the Details screen, the second to the last line is a link which says, "Add word to vocabulary".</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-add-to-vocab.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Clicking this line brings up a drop-down menu which allows you to add the word to a Vocabulary list.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-dropdown.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>The first line of the list always says, "Bulk Add Field".  Choosing this line will place the word in the "Bulk Add" field on the Vocabulary Creator page where you can decide what vocabulary group to add it to later.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details-word-added.png"></p>

<h3>Logeion iOS App</h3>
<p spacebelow=10>If you are on an iOS device, you will have one other option for word lookups.  The Logeion Website is also available as an iOS app.  If you have the Logeion iOS app installed, when you click to look up a word, you will see the additional option, "Logeion iOS App".</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details_lookup_ios.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you are on an iOS device and you do not see the "Logeion iOS App" option, then you probably don't have the app installed.  Click on the gear icon to bring up the Settings dialog box.  The Logeion iOS App will be disabled.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details_lookup_ios_disabled.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Click on the switch next to the name to enable it.  If it enables, then you are good to go.  But probably you will see the following message...</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details_lookup_nologeion.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you click "OK" then nothing will happen.  But if you click the "Install Logeion" button, then the program will open the iOS App Store to the Logeion app so you can install it.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details_logeion_appstore.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>After you have installed Logeion, try again to enable the line in the Setting dialog.  This time it should work.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details_logeion_enabled.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Now you can click on the "Logeion iOS App" line in the list of available sites, and it will take you immediately to the entry for that word in the Logeion App on your device.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/details_logeion_logos.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Logeion is a wonderful resource (both the App and on the Web) because it contains several excellent Ancient Greek dictionaries including the venerable "Liddell-Scott-Jones" (LSJ) as well as the "Abbott-Smith NT".</p>
<p spacebelow=10>Having Logeion on your device means lookups are almost instantaneous as you do not need to wait for the web browser to connect to a website.  And you never need worry about connectivity issues.  If you have an iOS device, I encourage you to get Logeion.  Because of its speed, I use it whenever I want a fuller definition of a word than the gloss which this app provides.</p>
