<h3>Creating New Vocabulary Lists</h3>
<p spacebelow=10>There are two ways to create a new vocabulary list: manually or by importing a list that someone else has shared.  Here we will show how to create a new list yourself.  See the section "Sharing Vocabulary" on the "About" page in order to see how to import a list that someone else has shared with you.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>To create a new vocabulary list, first you must make a new empty list.  On the vocabulary page, click the "Edit" button in the upper right to put the page into EDIT mode. Then click on the Plus sign ("+") which appears in the upper left to create a new list.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-parentlist-create.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Choose "Create New List", and then enter a name for the new list in the dialog box which appears.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-parentlist-new.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>The new list will be added to the top of the vocabulary lists, but you can reorder it at any time by dragging its reorder bars into a new position.  Here we have just created a new vocabulary list called "Mark".</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-parentlist-new-mark.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Click the "Done" button in the upper right to take the vocab screen out of EDIT mode.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-parentlist-new-normal.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Now click on the vocabulary list you just created to go to the Vocabulary groups for that list.  Since this is a brand new list and we have not created any groups, the list is empty.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-empty.png"></p>
<h3>Creating Vocabulary Groups in a List</h3>
<p spacebelow=10>Like with vocabulary lists, you can create vocabulary groups in two way: manually or by importing a vocabulary group which has been shared with you.  Here we will look here at how to create new groups manually.  Click on the "Edit" button in the upper right to put the page into EDIT mode.  Then click the Plus sign ("+") in the upper left.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-menu.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Choose "Create New Group", and you will go the Vocabulary Group Creator page.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-creator.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>There are two ways on this page to create a vocabulary group.  You can select words as a whole by frequency or you can add individual words.</p>
<h3>Create Vocabulary Groups by Frequency</h3>
<p spacebelow=10>To create a new group based on word frequency, select the "Frequency List" button near the top of the screen. (<i>This is the default choice when you create a new list.</i>)</p>
<p spacebelow=10>Click where it says "All Books" in below the title "Select words from:".  Here we choose which books of the Bible we want to select words from.  We can select words from "All Books" (the entire Greek New Testament), or we can select words from any individual book.  In this example we want to make vocabulary groups from the words in the book of Mark, so we choose "Mark" from the drop-down menu.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-bookmenu.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you choose a book, then you can also choose to select words from the entire book or from only one chapter.  Click on the word "All" below the heading labeled "Chapter:".  This brings up the chapter menu.  You can select "All" for the entire book (the default) or choose a particular chapter.  In the image below, we are choosing chapter 1 of Mark.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-chpmenu.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Then from the drop-down menu labeled "Word types:" we choose which types of words we want to select.  We can choose "All Words", or we can limit our selection to only verbs, only nouns, only adjectives, or only "miscellaneous" (which is everything other than verbs, nouns, and adjectives).</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-typemenu.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Next we choose the frequency of words which we want to select.  We can set a minimum frequency and a maximum frequency for how often a word appears in what we have selected. Click on either the "1" or the "All" below the "Minimum" and Maximum" labels to bring up the sliders.  (<i>Clicking on either brings up the same two sliders.</i>)</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-sliders.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>You can adjust the slider by clicking and dragging them.  You can also click and hold on the slider above or below the slider bar to move quickly through the values.  Finally, you can click and hold on the labels, "Minimum" and "Maximum", or the actual values (in this case "1" and "All") to move slowly up or down.  Adjust the sliders to choose how often a word must appear in the selection to be included in this group and click "OK" when you are done.  You'll see that the Minimum and Maximum columns now they the range that you just selected. (<i>The sliders are "inclusive", so if you chose 10 to 12, then you will see all words which appear 10, 11 or 12 times in the selection.</i>)</p>
<p spacebelow=10>In this case, we'll leave the sliders at "1" and "All".  So our settings are Book: Mark, Chapter: 1, Words: All Words, Frequency in Selection: 1 to All. Now click the button which says "Create List" and the program will pull out every word which appears in Mark chapter 1</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-mark-1.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>As you can see, there 239 unique words which appear Mark chapter 1.  By default, the list of words is in Alphabetical order, but you can change that by clicking on the line which says "Sort Results by:"  Let's change the sort order to "Count descending" which means we will see the words in order from most to least common.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-sort.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Now we that the most common word is the definite article, followed by the conjunction καί, followed by the pronoun αὐτός, and so on.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-sorted-count.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Below the line labeled "Frequency in Selection", there's a checkbox which says "Frequency in all books".  At present it is not checked, so there's no numbers under the Minimum and Maximum columns.  But if we check that box, those columns will become a range we can click like we can "Frequency in Selection".</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-allbooks.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>We use this range to limit the words which are chosen by the Frequency in Selection range.  Imagine that we already know our Greek vocabulary down to every word which appears 50 times or more in the New Testament.  In that case, many of the words in our Mark 1 list will be words we already know.  We want to study all the words in Mark 1 which do not appear <i>anywhere</i> in the Greek New Testament 50 or more times.  That's what this range allows us to do.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>So let's leave our "Frequency in Selection" range at 1 to All, but let's click on the "1" or "All" next to "Frequency in All Books" and use the sliders to change that range to 1 to 49.  (Remember that ranges are <i>inclusive</i>.  So if you set the range from 1 to 50, then you would get words which appeared 50 times in the entire New Testament.  We want words that appear <i>less</i> than 50 times, so we enter 49.)</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-sliders-1-49.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Now when we click "Create List", we see that there are 117 words in Mark 1 which appears less than 50 times anywhere in the Greek New Testament.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-less-than-49.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>If we are not happy with this list, we can reset our parameters and click the "Create List" item again.  Or we can remove words from this list by scrolling to the words we want to remove and clicking on the red trash icon to delete them.  Additionally we can add individual words to this list of words, either now or later after we have created it, using the "Manual Add" item above, and we can edit entries as well.  (See section "Adding Words Manually" later in this document.)</p>
<p spacebelow=10>If we are happy with our list, then let's save it as a vocabulary group (or groups) so we can study these words.  Click the "Save" button in the upper right to bring up the Save dialog box.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-save-dialog.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>First we must give the group a name.  We'll call this "Mark 1".  Then below where we enter the name, it asks you how many words to put in each group.  By default it will create 1 group with all the words from your list.  But if your list had hundreds (or thousands) of words, that could be far too large a group of words to study at one time.  Use the slider to tell the program what's the most words you want in any vocabulary group.  You can have up to 99 groups.  In this case, let's create four groups, which will have 29 or 30 words in each group.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-save-groups.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you create more than 1 group, you need to tell the program how to split the words up into those separate groups.  Click next to the label "Split Groups by:" where it says "Click here".  This allows you to split the words into groups alphabetically up or down, by count (how often the word appears in the selection) up or down, or randomly.  We'll choose randomly.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-sortby.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Once you have everything set the way you want, click the "Create" button, and the program will create the groups, place them on the Vocabulary Groups page, and take us back to that page. So here we see the two groups we created from the words in Mark 1.  Notice that the program has taken the name we gave it, "Mark 1", and appended "-01" and "-02" to distinguish the two groups.  But we can change the names of the groups to whatever we want.  (See the section on "Editing Vocabulary" in the "About" page for information on how to edit the names of lists and groups.)</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-mark1.png"></p>
<h3>Save By Chapters</h3>
<p spacebelow=10>There is one other Save option that can be helpful here.  If you choose words from "All" chapters of a book, then when you go to save the groups, the checkbox under the Name field becomes active.  This allows you to create groups for every chapter in the book without having to create a group for each chapter manually.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>Let's do this whole process again to demonstrate this.  Here on the Vocabulary Groups page, click the "Edit" button in the upper right, then click the Plus sign ("+") in the upper left, then choose "Create New Group" from the menu which appears.  This takes us back to the Vocabulary Groups Creator page.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>On that page we select Mark, but this time we select "All" chapters instead of a specific chapter.  We select "All Words", and we leave the "Frequency in Selection" to 1 to All.  But let's change the "Frequency in All Books" to 1 to 9 (so we get less words this way).  So our screen looks like this:</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-mark-all.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Click the "Create List" link, and we see that there are 549 words in the entire book of Mark which appear less than 10 times in the entire Greek New Testament.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-mark-total.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Now when we click the "Save" button and go to the Save dialog, we see that the checkbox "Save Words by chapters" is active and can be checked.  (It was not active when we saved words from a single chapter.)</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-chaptersave-active.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Check that box, and you will see that the name is filled in for you as the name of the book (you cannot edit this name), and the slider below changes.  Previously the default was to create one big group with all the words.  But now the program is going to automatically create a group of words for each chapter.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-chaptersave.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Since Mark has 16 chapters, the program will create, at most, 16 groups, one for each chapter.  We need to tell it whether we want those groups to have a maximum number of words.  The default is to create a single group for each chapter.  So if there are 12 words in a chapter, that group will have 12 words in it.  If there are 100 words in a chapter, that group will have 100 words in it.  But you can use this slider to limit how big a group can be.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>If you do choose to limit how many words can appear in a group, then like before, you'll need to tell the program how to split the words into group by clicking on the "Split Groups by" item.  In this case, we will leave the default of one group per chapter.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>I don't want more than 30 words in any group, so I'm going to limit the groups to maximum 30 words, and if there are multiple groups per chapter, I'll assign those words to groups randomly.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-chaptersave-30max.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Now when you click the "Create" button, the program will go through the list of words and pull out all the words which are found in chapter 1 of Mark and put them in a group.  Then it will go through all the words in chapter 2 which do not appear in chapter 1 and put them in a group.  So the chapter 2 group will not have any duplicate words from chapter 1.  Then it will go through the list and pull out all the words which appear in chapter 3 but not in chapters 1 or 2 and put them into a group.  It will do this all the way through the entire book.  So all the words in your list will be placed in a group for the chapter that word first appears in.  But no word will ever appear twice even if it appears in multiple chapters.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>When it is done, it will take you back to the Vocabulary Groups page and show you the groups it created.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-mark-all-chp.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>As you can see, the program has created groups for each chapter in Mark.  Since chapter one has 41 words, it's been split over two group - 30 words in the first group and but only 11 in the next  Then the next two chapters only have 23 words each, so they are each a single group.  Chapter 4 has 36 words, so it's split into two groups, and so on.  If you add up the totals of the number of words in each chapter, it will equal 549 because there were 549 words in the list we created of words in Mark which appear less than 10 times in the entire Greek New Testament.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>One thing to note is that saving by chapters only works with frequency lists made from an entire book.  If you make a list from the entire New Testament or from a single chapter in a book, the option to "Save words by chapters" will be grayed out and you can't enable it.  The same is true if you edit any words in a frequency list.  So if you want to make a vocabulary list for a book by chapter, make the frequency list and save it.  Don't make any changes to it.</p>

<h3>Adding Vocabulary Manually</h3>
<p spacebelow=10>You can create a list of any words or forms you wish, and you can add entries to any frequency list you have created, by clicking on the "Manual Add" at the top of the page.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-mark-manual.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>You can search for dictionary words by selecting "Words" from the chooser at the top.  Or you can search for forms of words that appear in the Greek New Testament by choosing "Forms".  As you type, the program will output Greek characters.  You can see what Latin characters correspond to which Greek characters by long pressing on the Greek keyboard indicator or the "Ω" symbol at the end of the field.  (See the section "Typing in Greek" on the "About" page for more information on how to type Greek characters.)</p>
<p spacebelow=10>As you type, words (or forms depending on which you have choosen) will appear.  Clicking on one will put it into the fields where you can edit it.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>So in the image below I have typed "agw" into the search field which the program reads as "αγω" and shows me all the words in the dictionary which begin with these letters.  There are five as you can see.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-manual-word-agw.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Now I'll click to choose "Forms" from the chooser.  The program shows me any form of any word in the New Testament which begins with those letters.  You can see the word itself, what the root of the word is (in parentheses next to the word) and how it is parsed.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-manual-form-agw.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>In the image below, I have clicked on the third entry, "ἄγωμεν (ἄγω)".  The form, ἄγωμεν, has been put into the top "Greek" field, and the parsing (now noting what verb it's from) has been put into the lower "Gloss" field.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-manual-agwmen.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Both these fields can now be edited.  By default the built-in Greek keyboard is enabled for the "Greek" field and disabled for the "Gloss" field.  But you can enable or disable the Greek keyboard on either field by clicking the Greek keyboard indicator to toggle it on and off.  (See the "Typing in Greek" section on the "About" page for more information about how the built-in Greek keyboard works.)</p>
<p spacebelow=10>Once you have edited the fields as you wish, press the "Add Entry" button and that word or form will be added to your list of words.  In the image below, I have added the entry for ἄγωμεν without changing anything.  Since the list was empty, this is the only entry in it.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-manual-agwmen-alone.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>But if there had already been words in the list, then this entry would have been added to it.  Below you can see this form added to our list of words from Mark 1 which appear less than 10 times in the entire Greek New Testament.  (Note that the program will not add duplicate entries.  But you can add the same word/form with a different gloss.)</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-manual-agwmen-inlist.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>In addition to adding individual words and forms using the "Manual Add" feature, you can also add ALL forms of a word found in the Greek New Testament.  Choose "All Forms" from the chooser near the top and then type a word into the search field.  Below I have typed "agw" again into the search field.  And just like when searching for "Words", we see all the dictionary words in the Greek New Testament which begin with these letters.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-allforms-agw.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>But if we click on one of these words, rather than putting the data into the fields so you can edit them, the program will put ALL forms of this word found in the Greek New Testament into the word list.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>So below we have clicked on the word "ἄγω".  After asking us to confirm the additions, we now see all forms of this verb which are found in the Greek New Testament.  There 28 forms of the word "ἄγω" which appear in the text.  Moreover, in this view, the "Count" column is the number of times this <i>form</i> appears in the text.  So "ἀγαγεῖν", the aorist active infinitive of "ἄγω" appears twice in the entire Greek New Testament.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-allforms-added-agw.png"></p>

<h3>Editing Entries</h3>
<p spacebelow=10>As noted above, you can delete any individual entry by clicking on the red Trash icon for that line.  But if you click anywhere else on the line, the Greek text and gloss will be put into those fields on the "Manual Add" screen, and you can edit them.  In the image below, we have clicked on the line "ἄγριος, α, ον".  The Greek text and the gloss can now be edited in the appropriate fields.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-edit-agrios.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Once you have edited the entry, click the "Save Entry" button and the edited values will be put back into the words list.  Please note that everything else on the card is disabled while you are editing.  You must either save the entry or press the "Cancel" button in order to access other functions on the screen.  (If you leave the page without saving the entry, any edits you made will be ignored.)</p>
<h3>Bulk Adding Entries</h3>
<p spacebelow=10>Finally, you can add multiple entries at once by clicking on the "Bulk Add" item in the chooser at the top of the screen.  This presents you with a field that you can type into and add entries <i>en masse</i>.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-bulkadd.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Simply type the words you want to add to your list, one word per line.  In the list below, we have typed a number of very common Greek verbs into the field.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-bulkadd-verbs.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Like many other fields in the program, there is a built-in Greek keyboard which you know is active by the gray "Gr" in the background.  (See "Typing in Greek" on the "About" page for more information.)  Notice that we did not have to type in accents or other diacritics.  The program tries to figure out what you mean.  Sometimes there are multiple possibilities for what word you meant.  For example, if you enter "εις", that could be the Greek number "εἷς" ("one") or the preposition "εἰς" ("into").  In that case, both words will added to your list, and you can scroll down and delete the one you do not want.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-multiples.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>If, however, the program simply cannot figure out what Greek word you mean, it will alert you and leave the word in the field for you to edit.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-bad-entry.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Click on the "Add Words" button, and the words you typed (assuming the program recognizes them) are put into the list of words.</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-bulkadd-verbs-added.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>You can also add forms which are not dictionary words by entering, not only the root word, but also the form and gloss you wish to be part of this vocabulary entry.  Enter "dictionary word::Greek::Gloss" and the program understands that the two colons (::) means you will provide the Greek form and the gloss that you want to use in this entry.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>So imagine that we want to enter a number second aorist forms.  One way would be to use the "Manual Add" section, search on the forms, and add them one by one, editing the glosses.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>But another way would be to add them here as custom entries.  So let's add an entry for "ἦλθον" as the 2nd aorist of "ἔρχομαι" and "βάλον" as the 2nd aorist of "βάλλω".  So we will add the following two lines to the Bulk Add field.  (See "Typing in Greek" on the "About" page for information how to type in Greek with diacritics.)</p>
<p leftindent=10><u>ερχομαι::ἦλθον::2nd aorist of ἔρχομαι</u></p>
<p leftindent=10 spacebelow=10><u>βαλλω::βάλον::2nd aorist of βάλλω</u></p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-bulkadd-entries.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>As you can see, the initial dictionary word does not need to have accents or diacritics.  The program will try to figure out what you mean.  But everything after the double colons (::) will appear in the vocabulary list exactly as you type it.  See "Typing in Greek" on the "About" page for information how to type in Greek with diacritics if you wish to do so.</p>
<p spacebelow=10>Click the "Add Words" button, and you see the custom entries have been added to our list exactly as you entered them.  (Please note that when you add words through the Bulk Add screen, the count is the count for the dictionary word, not the form, as it would be if you added the form through the "Manual Add" screen.)</p>
<p spacebelow=10><img src="binfile:help/images/vocab-childlist-bulkadd-added.png"></p>
<p spacebelow=10>Please see the section "Editing Vocabulary" on the "About" page for more information on how to edit words in and add words to existing vocabulary lists.</p>
